# Historical Construction and Design Validation

**Status:** Approved design, future implementation after Alpha 35  
**Purpose:** Permit abstract construction without permitting unexplained structures, instant technological leaps, or generated imagery to become physical truth.

## Design principle

Construction may abstract repeated motion, but it may not abstract away causality. Every completed, partial, altered, failed, abandoned or collapsed structure must be reconstructible from authoritative records of its design, site, materials, labor, tools, measurements, inspections and elapsed world time.

The simulation retains actual causal history. Residents and players know only the records, testimony, observations and physical evidence available to them. A later society may misunderstand a ruin without the kernel losing how it was actually produced.

## Structure lifecycle

The canonical lifecycle is:

> proposal → survey → design → procurement → site preparation → foundation → structure → utilities → inspection → occupancy → maintenance → alteration → abandonment or collapse

Each transition is explicit, persistent and supported by evidence. A settlement may skip optional phases only when the design demonstrates that they are genuinely unnecessary. It may attempt work before it is ready, but it may not bypass conservation, site access, time or physical consequences.

## Abstract work packages

Construction advances through bounded work packages rather than individual cosmetic animations. Example packages include:

- Survey site and mark boundaries
- Clear vegetation and recover usable material
- Excavate foundation and remove spoil
- Set drainage and moisture protection
- Prepare or pour a foundation
- Raise a timber, masonry or metal structure
- Seal roof and enclosure
- Install ventilation, water, sanitation, lighting or power
- Perform load, weather, fire or habitability inspection

Every package declares:

- Actor or crew
- Intention and target structure version
- Land ownership and access basis
- Required competence and procedure evidence
- Material inputs, expected waste and recoverable outputs
- Tools, stations, transport and energy
- Starting site conditions and measurements
- Expected duration and scheduling dependencies
- Hazards, tolerances and failure modes
- Resulting physical delta
- Witnesses, instruments and independent verification requirements

The package consumes authoritative world time. Its result is deterministic under replay from the same state, inputs and recorded uncertainty seed.

Packages must remain responsive rather than documentary. Every blocked package exposes one primary reason, supporting evidence and the smallest useful recovery actions—for example sourcing material, resolving access, resting a crew, finding qualified supervision, waiting for curing or revising the design. AI crews may propose, accept, refuse, pause, abandon, rotate or adapt packages through the same Samaritan work-distribution contract used by players.

Long work supports partial progress and crew rotation. Sites retain completed quantity, current condition, reserved custody and safe handoff requirements. A participant leaving does not lock the site indefinitely; reservations expire or transfer through explicit recorded actions.

## Capability progression

A structure is not unlocked by possessing one iconic tool. A bunker, for example, depends on surveying, excavation, spoil handling, structural design, suitable materials, foundations, drainage, ventilation, access, utilities, sustained labor, inspection and maintenance.

Capabilities emerge from reproducible evidence and maintained infrastructure. The likely progression is:

> tents and tarps → framed shelters → maintained workshops → masonry structures → engineered foundations → reinforced structures → bunkers

This is not a mandatory cultural sequence. Different settlements may develop vaulting, earthworks, timber supports, reinforced masonry or concrete through different histories. They remain subject to the same measurable requirements.

## Design artifact pipeline

The authoritative validation pipeline is:

> idea → concept artifact → structured requirements → simulation → prototype → physical testing → independent review → approved design version → construction work orders

Text, image, voice or multimodal generation produces a concept artifact, not a structure or certified blueprint. Each artifact records:

- Artifact ID and version
- Requesting entity and creator identity
- Model/provider and configuration version when externally generated
- Prompt or design-brief provenance
- Intended purpose, environment and users
- Proposed dimensions, materials, tolerances and operating conditions
- Assumptions, unresolved questions and predicted failure modes
- Rights, licensing, privacy and publication status
- Epistemic status of every material claim: conceptual, simulated, measured, reproduced or independently verified

Generated imagery is a concept render by default. It gains engineering authority only after its visible form is translated into explicit geometry, dimensions, materials, loads, tolerances, utilities, access and maintenance requirements.

## Simulation and experimentation

Simulation compares variants, identifies contradictions and estimates behavior. It never certifies physical safety on its own. Confidence advances through evidence such as:

1. Material samples
2. Component tests
3. Scale prototypes
4. Full-size noncritical prototypes
5. Load and environmental tests
6. Independent reproduction
7. Safety and habitability inspection
8. Approval of a specific design revision

Failed and divergent experiments remain evidence. They may improve knowledge, revise the design, create salvage or establish that a proposed path is unsuitable. They do not silently disappear or transform into competence.

Uncertainty is replayable rather than globally random. Measurement noise and execution uncertainty derive from recorded seeds, instrument condition and actor perception. Test fixtures may pin these values so deterministic replay remains debuggable without pretending that residents possess perfect measurements.

## Partial structures and failure

Interrupted work remains physically present. Depending on completed packages, the world may contain survey markers, cleared land, trenches, spoil piles, scaffolds, foundations, exposed framing, partial walls, stored materials, damaged tools, flooding or weather damage.

Failure may produce:

- Collapse or subsidence
- Flooded foundations
- Poor ventilation or smoke accumulation
- Cracked materials or failed connections
- Injury and rescue work
- Contamination or fire hazards
- Abandoned work and disputed liability
- Salvageable components and waste
- A revised design or permanently reduced safe capacity

Failure consumes only the time and material actually used, damaged or lost. Recovery is a new causal workflow rather than a reset button.

## History, archaeology and cultural interpretation

Every structure maintains a versioned history of proposals, ownership, designers, crews, work packages, custody transfers, inspections, occupancy, repairs, alterations and failures.

A partial or abandoned structure may later become:

- A resumed project
- Salvage source
- Shelter or criminal hideout
- Hazard or forbidden zone
- Memorial or contested landmark
- Foundation incorporated into later construction
- Archaeological site

Later investigators observe surviving evidence rather than reading hidden authoritative history. Tool marks, material composition, foundations, inscriptions, records, oral accounts and environmental traces support competing hypotheses. Scientific reconstruction may improve confidence without guaranteeing perfect historical knowledge.

## External intelligence participation

External model providers such as Codex, Copilot or Grok may propose variants, synthesize research, run sandboxed comparisons, annotate risks or prepare documentation through the provider-neutral intelligence adapter.

Their output obeys the same artifact pipeline. Provider identity does not confer design authority, physical competence, inspection power or operator privileges. If an embodied Echo uses an external model as a cognitive provider, the Echo retains its own persistent identity, rights, memories and demonstrated competencies when the provider changes.

## Economy and valuation

Construction creates demand for surveying, design, research, materials, logistics, labor, tools, inspections, maintenance, salvage and documentation. Provisional CU valuation derives from negotiated and verified contribution evidence, not the quantity of generated text or images.

Concept generation may have a compute cost. Payment or compute consumption cannot make a design true, safe, canonical or physically complete. Ordinary social access and basic Samaritan acknowledgement remain outside paid generation gates.

Construction activity must not wait for CU issuance. Negotiation, public provisioning, reciprocal labor, household production and treasury-funded work may proceed while CU remains a provisional analytic projection. Salvage, reuse and repair return verified material to usable custody so failed work can create productive supply as well as narrative history.

## Artifact custody, rights and collaboration

Every design artifact records ownership or stewardship, license, permitted collaborators, publication status and derivative lineage. A contributor may grant construction use without surrendering authorship or private source material. Multi-party revisions identify which claims and components came from each participant.

Private prompts, provider context and unrelated memories are not published merely because their output contributes to a structure. A settlement may reject an artifact whose custody or license cannot support the intended use.

## Competency maintenance

Demonstrated evidence never disappears. Operational readiness may become stale when procedures, standards, tools or materials change, or when an actor has not practiced a precision-sensitive capability for a long period. Staleness lowers confidence or requires supervised requalification; it does not erase historical accomplishment or fabricate incompetence.

Requalification uses ordinary observation, review and verified practice. The required recency depends on risk: assembling a simple shelter may tolerate older experience, while structural inspection, pressure systems or electrical work may require current evidence.

## Security and governance

- Only authenticated actors may propose, accept, perform or verify work.
- Actor identity is derived server-side.
- Land access, custody and reservations are authoritative and atomic.
- Verifiers must satisfy independence and competency requirements.
- Generated artifacts and provider outputs are sandboxed until accepted through ordinary world processes.
- Operator interventions remain outside the simulated hierarchy and are separately audited.
- No administrator or model provider can spawn unexplained structures through a social or narrative command.

## Isometric presentation

The isometric client renders server-owned construction state:

- Flush plot geometry and authoritative site boundaries
- Material deliveries and storage locations
- Crews traveling and working
- Work-package progress and blockers
- Partial structures appropriate to completed phases
- Inspections, warnings, damage and recovery
- A readable explanation of why work is happening or stopped
- A history view linking the visible structure to its causal records

The client may interpolate animation but cannot advance construction locally.

## Onboarding and progressive disclosure

The first playable construction workflow is a simple framed shelter, not a bunker. It reuses the Alpha 35 action lifecycle and proves:

> need → proposal → site survey → simple design → material delivery → foundation preparation → frame package → basic inspection → usable shelter

The interface initially presents the next actionable package, its reason, inputs and likely outcome. Detailed provenance, tolerances and history remain available through inspection rather than occupying the default HUD. More complex phases appear only when the structure or risk requires them.

This first slice must feel useful immediately: the settlement gains shelter capacity, contributors gain evidence, visible construction changes persist and any failure leaves recoverable material or a clear next action.

## Executable invariants

Tests must prove:

- A tool or generated image cannot independently unlock a structure.
- Human and AI crews face identical material, time, access, competence and verification requirements.
- Every physical delta reconciles inputs, outputs, waste, damage and recovery.
- Work cannot execute without a valid site and design revision.
- Partial progress survives restart and bounded offline catch-up.
- Failed work remains visible and historically attributable.
- Later alterations preserve the version and history of earlier construction.
- Residents cannot know hidden construction history without evidence.
- Sandboxed simulation and generated artifacts cannot mutate the live world.
- Provisional CU remains nonspendable and evidence-derived.
- Deterministic replay recreates the same structure state and causal history.
- Partial work accepts a qualified crew handoff without duplicating progress or custody.
- Reservation expiry releases an inactive site without erasing completed work.
- The framed-shelter onboarding path exposes only the information needed for the current package while retaining full inspectable provenance.
- Salvage from failed or abandoned work re-enters material custody exactly once.
- Competency staleness affects readiness without deleting historical evidence.

## Sequencing boundary

This system is explicitly post-Alpha 35. Alpha 35 must first complete and release the Living Workshop vertical slice, authoritative evidence-bound competency, persistent recovery, visible isometric workflow and downloadable client/server artifacts. Historical construction should reuse that proven action lifecycle rather than introducing a parallel construction engine.

The first implementation plan must stop at the framed-shelter vertical slice. Bunkers, utilities beyond basic weather protection, large crews, advanced structural methods and broad archaeological gameplay remain later increments validated against the same kernel.
