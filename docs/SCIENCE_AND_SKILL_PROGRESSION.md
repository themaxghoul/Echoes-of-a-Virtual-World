# Science and skill progression

EoV will not require every player or new AI mind to begin with smithing. Smithing is one optional, needs-driven applied branch and a useful later vertical sliceâ€”not the universal tutorial.

The existing fantasy-oriented skill router remains legacy architecture. Core simulation progression uses evidence-bearing competencies with six independent dimensions:

- theory;
- direct observation;
- procedural knowledge;
- embodied execution;
- reproducibility;
- teaching.

Reading a physics text can raise theory. It cannot create laboratory observation, motor practice or a reproduced result. A confident entity can therefore know explanations while remaining unable to execute the work.

## Perspective rules

### Story/text

Permitted directly:

- read and write records;
- form hypotheses;
- discuss evidence;
- plan procedures;
- negotiate and delegate work;
- teach theory;
- review submitted measurements;
- publish or dispute findings.

Indirect only:

- physical experiments;
- fabrication;
- cooking;
- smithing;
- terrain modification;
- tool operation.

Text instructions may cause another autonomous entity to choose an action, but that entity remains the physical actor and uses its own competence, tools and observations.

### Isometric 2.5D

- inspect spatial systems and resource flows;
- reserve stations and inputs;
- issue, supervise, interrupt and verify work orders;
- manipulate task-specific controls where the action is visible;
- run instruments and experiments represented in the simulation;
- gain strong procedural and supervisory competence, with reduced embodied gain where fine motion is abstracted.

### First person

- directly handle tools and instruments;
- perceive sound, surface detail, occlusion and nearby hazards;
- acquire full embodied-practice evidence;
- perform close inspection unavailable from the overview.

VR remains deferred and eventually maps to the first-person action contract.

## Initial domain graph

Foundation: measurement.

Sciences: mathematics, physics, chemistry and biology.

Applied sciences: materials science and agriculture.

Engineering: mechanical, electrical and civil.

Crafts: cooking and smithing.

Institutional competencies: logistics and administration.

Domains have prerequisites, but prerequisites permit attempts rather than guarantee results. Failed experiments and divergent reproductions remain valuable evidence.

## Advancement rules

- Domain competence starts at zero unless acquired in the seed's recorded prehistory.
- Advancement requires evidence events in the Causal Ledger.
- Theory alone never unlocks direct professional reliability.
- Repeated unverified practice produces slow learning and possible bad habits.
- Independent matched reproduction raises scientific reliability.
- Divergent reproduction raises observation while lowering certainty about a claim.
- Teachers cannot transfer more competence than they have demonstrated.
- Apprentices may act under supervision before independent qualification.
- Tool design and experimental results retain model, instrument and material provenance.
- Titles describe demonstrated history; they do not grant abilities.

## Revised implementation order

1. Competency dimensions, domain graph and evidence records.
2. Perspective-specific observation and action gates.
3. Measurement as the common introductory practical skill.
4. Basic survival choices and needs-based task discovery.
5. Scientific observation, hypothesis and reproduction loop.
6. Teaching and apprenticeship.
7. Optional applied branches: cooking, agriculture, repair, logistics and administration.
8. Smithing vertical slice once materials science, heat and measurement exist.
9. Electrical and mechanical engineering workflows.
10. Broader interdisciplinary development and potential generalization research.
